
Let’s streamline this into absolutely critical steps to start taking on clients and receiving money. I’ll also provide tools and resources for each, keeping it as lean and cost-effective as possible.

Critical Steps to Start Taking on Clients

1. Create an LLC
	•	Why: Protects your personal assets by separating your business and personal liabilities.
	•	How to Do It:
	1.	Go to the Texas Secretary of State’s website.
	•	URL: Texas SOS Direct
	2.	File a Certificate of Formation (Form 205).
	•	Filing Fee: $300.
	3.	Choose a name for your LLC. Use the Texas SOS Direct Name Availability Search to ensure it’s not taken.
	4.	Appoint a registered agent (you can act as your own agent in Texas).
	•	Lean Tools:
	•	Use a platform like ZenBusiness or IncFile for step-by-step help.
	•	Cost: $49–$150 (plus state fees).

2. Obtain an EIN
	•	Why: Needed for tax purposes and to open a business bank account.
	•	How to Do It:
	1.	Go to the IRS EIN Application website.
	•	URL: IRS EIN Application
	2.	Apply online (free).
	3.	You’ll receive your EIN immediately upon completion.

3. Open a Business Bank Account
	•	Why: Keeps personal and business finances separate, simplifies taxes, and builds credibility.
	•	How to Do It:
	1.	Use your LLC Certificate of Formation and EIN to open an account.
	2.	Recommended banks for small businesses:
	•	Chase: Free account with a $2,000 minimum balance.
	•	Bluevine: Free online banking with no minimums.
	3.	Set up your account to accept direct deposits and wire transfers.

4. Set Up Payment Processing
	•	Why: Enables you to invoice clients and accept payments seamlessly.
	•	How to Do It:
	1.	Choose a payment processor:
	•	Stripe: Best for card payments, integrates with websites.
	•	PayPal: Trusted by clients, especially for one-off payments.
	•	Square: Great for recurring invoices.
	2.	Create a free account and link it to your business bank account.
	3.	Test the workflow to ensure you can invoice and receive payments.

5. Draft a Standard Client Contract
	•	Why: Protects you legally, sets clear terms, and ensures you get paid.
	•	How to Do It:
	1.	Use templates from trusted platforms like Rocket Lawyer or LawDepot (cost: ~$20–$50).
	2.	Include:
	•	Payment terms (e.g., upfront deposit, cancellation fees).
	•	Ownership of deliverables (e.g., retain copyright until full payment).
	•	Scope of work and timelines.
	3.	Keep it simple but professional.

Optional But Helpful Steps
	•	Set Up Invoicing Software: Use free tools like Wave to create professional invoices and track payments.
	•	Business Insurance: General liability insurance is helpful but not critical to get started.

Minimal Tools and Costs
	1.	LLC Formation: $300 (Texas SOS Direct) or ~$350 (with ZenBusiness or IncFile).
	2.	EIN: Free (IRS website).
	3.	Business Bank Account: Free (e.g., Bluevine or Chase with minimum balance).
	4.	Payment Processing: Free to set up; processing fees (~2.9% + $0.30 per transaction).
	5.	Client Contract Templates: $20–$50 (Rocket Lawyer, LawDepot).

Prioritized Checklist
	1.	File for your LLC (Texas SOS Direct).
	2.	Obtain your EIN (IRS website).
	3.	Open a business bank account.
	4.	Set up payment processing (Stripe/PayPal).
	5.	Draft a simple client contract.

FAQs About Streamlining

Do I Need a CPA?

No, you don’t need a CPA to create an LLC or EIN. These are straightforward tasks you can handle yourself using the websites above. However, consider hiring a CPA later to help with quarterly tax filings.

How Can I Keep Costs Low?
	•	Do the LLC and EIN yourself to save on service fees.
	•	Use free or low-cost tools for invoicing, contracts, and payment processing.

Can I Delay Some Steps?
	•	You can technically start without forming an LLC by operating as a sole proprietor, but this exposes you to personal liability.
	•	Focus on the LLC, EIN, and bank account first—these are foundational.
