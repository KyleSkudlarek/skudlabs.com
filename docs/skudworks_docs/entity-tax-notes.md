## DISSOLUTION — full checklist

Three authorities. **Comptroller first, SOS second** (SOS won't terminate until you're tax-clear); IRS letter
can go anytime.

### 1. IRS (federal) — just a letter
Per the IRS call, **no 1120 is required** given zero activity. Mail a signed letter to close the business account:
- **Include:** EIN (33-2690671), business name (SKUDWORKS), address, a sentence stating you're no longer operating
  and want to close the account; sign + date.
- **Mail to:** Internal Revenue Service, **STOP 6273, Ogden, UT 84201**.
- **Certified mail** optional but worth the few dollars for proof. Processing ~30 days.

### 2. TX Comptroller (first; free) — produces the clearance the SOS needs
- **Public Information Report — Form 05-102:** annual disclosure (member/agent/address). No franchise tax due
  under the ~$2.47M threshold, but the PIR is still required. File via **Webfile** at comptroller.texas.gov;
  file the **final** franchise report (mark final), PIR included.
  - Need the entity's **Webfile number** ("XT…") — on the Comptroller letter, or call **800-252-1381**.
- **Certificate of Account Status to Terminate — Form 05-359:** the tax-clearance doc. Request via Webfile after
  the final report is filed. Free. **Allow ~2–4 weeks to issue.**

### 3. TX Secretary of State — legally ends the entity ($40)
- **Certificate of Termination — Form 651:** download from sos.state.tx.us or file via **SOSDirect**.
- **Attach the 05-359** (SOS rejects it without the clearance).
- **Fee $40**, paid to the Secretary of State (SOSDirect card +2.7%, or check by mail).
- Once accepted, Skudworks legally ceases to exist.

## NEW ENTITY — Skud Labs LLC (DIY, ~$300)

- File **Certificate of Formation (Form 205)** with TX SOS via SOSDirect, **$300**. Be your own registered agent.
- **Leave it as a plain single-member LLC — do NOT file Form 2553 or 8832.** Default = disregarded = simple
  Schedule C, no payroll, no separate federal return. (This is the mistake to avoid this time.)
- Get a **new EIN** (free, irs.gov) — do NOT reuse Skudworks's EIN.
- Then: **business bank account** (needs EIN + formation docs) → **Stripe** (payouts to that account).

## Contracts / legal (separate from the CPA — needs a lawyer or template)

- CPAs can't draft contracts (that's law). The "don't get screwed" stack = **services contract (MSA) + SOW +
  IP-transfers-only-on-full-payment clause + 50% deposit**. NDA is usually folded into the MSA, not the main event.
- **Sign as the LLC in representative capacity** ("Skud Labs LLC, by Kyle Skudlarek, Managing Member") — never
  personally (protects the veil). Get paid **to the LLC's account**.
- Cheap path: vetted template (Rocket Lawyer/LawDepot/Bonsai) + optional 1-hr business-attorney review (~$200–500),
  then reuse per client. E-signature (DocuSign/Dropbox Sign) is legally binding (ESIGN/UETA) — no special tier; it's
  for the audit trail, not legality. Don't start work before signed contract + deposit clears.
- Have a **W-9** ready (LLC name + EIN) for business clients who'll 1099 you. Report all income on Schedule C
  regardless of whether a 1099 arrives. (W-2 = Cigna; 1099-NEC = clients — different forms.)

## Texas sales tax (CPA question — TX-specific, real)
- You're **in-state → physical nexus from $0**; the $500k threshold (remote sellers) does NOT shield you. No
  in-state volume minimum.
- Taxability depends on what/where: **out-of-state clients = no TX tax** (won't hit other states' ~$100k nexus at
  your volume). TX clients: **website build/hosting/SaaS = taxable** (data processing; SaaS taxed on 80%);
  **custom software = NOT taxable if you transfer exclusive rights**; pure design/consulting = not taxable.
- If taxable: get a **Sales & Use Tax Permit**, add tax **on top** of price (client pays it), remit to the
  **Comptroller** (one return; they distribute local) on an assigned schedule (small sellers usually quarterly/annual).

## Deductions — quick reference
- **No profit required to deduct.** Real business (profit motive) can run a loss that offsets W-2 income.
- A single-member LLC isn't a separate return — it's a **Schedule C on the 1040**.
- **Pre-launch = start-up costs (§195):** up to $5k year one, amortize rest over 15 yrs. Includes **formation +
  legal/CPA setup fees**.
- **CPA/legal fees** for the business are deductible (formation portion = start-up cost; only the business share of
  1040 prep is deductible).
- **Home office:** exclusive + regular use; simplified ($5/sqft, max $1,500) or actual (% of rent). No loss creation.
- Pay everything from the **business bank account**; keep receipts.
