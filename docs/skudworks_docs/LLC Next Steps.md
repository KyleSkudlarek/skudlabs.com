1. Filing Form 2553 with the IRS:
    •   Purpose: Elect S-corp status for federal tax purposes.
    •   Deadline: File within 75 days of forming your business or starting operations. Late elections are allowed with “reasonable cause.”
    •   Steps:
    1.  Download Form 2553: Available on the IRS website. (https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-2553)
    2.  Complete Sections:
    •   Part I: General information (e.g., your EIN, incorporation date, and tax year).
    •   Part II: Only if you’re a Qualified Subchapter S Trust (most small businesses skip this).
    •   Part III: Late election request (if applicable).
    3.  Obtain Signatures: All shareholders (if applicable) must sign the form.
    4.  Submit:
    •   Fax or mail the completed form to the IRS office for your state (details in the form instructions).
    •   Alternatively, consult a tax professional for e-filing options.

2. Registering with the Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts:
    •   Purpose: Texas imposes no state income tax but requires franchise tax filings for most businesses.
    •   Steps:
    1.  Create a Webfile Account:
    •   Go to the Texas Comptroller website. (https://comptroller.texas.gov/) 
    •   Register your business for a Webfile Number using your EIN and Texas taxpayer ID.
    2.  File Franchise Tax Reports:
    •   Reports are due annually, even if you owe no tax (due May 15th each year for most entities).
    •   New businesses are exempt from franchise tax for the first year but must still file a “No Tax Due” report.
    3.  Maintain Compliance:
    •   Check deadlines for periodic reports to avoid penalties.

3. Ensuring Proper Setup with the Texas Secretary of State:
    •   Steps:
    1.  Formation Filing:
    •   Confirm that you filed the appropriate Certificate of Formation with the Texas Secretary of State when creating your entity.
    •   For an LLC, this is Form 205 (Certificate of Formation–LLC).
    •   For a corporation, this is Form 201 (Certificate of Formation–For-Profit Corporation).
    2.  Obtain a Certificate of Filing:
    •   This serves as proof your business is officially registered in Texas.
    3.  File the Public Information Report (PIR):
    •   Due annually. It ensures your business information is up to date.
    •   File through the Texas Comptroller when submitting franchise tax reports.
    4.  Registered Agent:
    •   Ensure your registered agent’s details are up-to-date. You can update this information by filing Form 401 (Change of Registered Agent).
